# What I learned today

Dude, today was weird. Why? I am perplexed at how an organization, lets call it... "Acme Corp", deals with internal communication. Explain to me how you can build a solution, plan to ship it next week, and I, one of the main engineers tasked with getting this solution implemented with the customers, am just now learning of this solution. The particulars are neither here nor there. The point is, when you build something... **PEOPLE SHOULD KNOW ABOUT IT**. Not just your fucking sponsor, your coworkers, and your boss. But your sister orgs should also at least be fucking aware of what you're building and understand why. They should see how it fits the narrative, the "roadmap", and how it delivers to the customer the solutions that moves the needle on their world. So what did I learn today? To broadcast what your building... lol if it matters, even just a little, and its not just an internal tool, fucking tell people. Not in an arrogant "look at me" way, but in a "this is important and here's why" way. Communication is key, and assuming everyone knows what you're doing is a recipe for confusion and missed opportunities. This reminds me of my research in teleoperation... I came up with a phrase all on my own (even though its a well known saying) and it goes: "You can't fix what you can't measure", and in this context, "You can't implement or market or bring to customers what you don't communicate". I get it, the lone engineer sitting in his cubicle never talking to anyone and just "doing the good work that keeps everything afloat" has a sort of mystical aura of competence and respect. In my experience, if that engineer just spent more time I don't know... just **talking** to people they would probably be better off. And no I am not some "climb the corporate ladder" junkie. I am just a ... ya know... "guy who believes in doing the best work...." and I have learned that it just so happens means talking to people, getting to know people, and telling people about yourself and what you are working on is not only good for internal alignment but also people just will generally like you better. You are a human. Where did we all get to this place where using emojis was unprofessional? I mean what IS *professional*? I think it comes down to getting a mission done: delivering a service or product to a customer to help them solve their problem. So who cares if you use GIFs to show your excitement over a new product release?? You **CARE**. And to me, thats the pre-requisite to fighting the "unprofessional" badge. If you do something and its because you care (like using emojis to show happiness) then who cares??

# Idk man I think thats it

Anyway, that's my rant for today. Hopefully, tomorrow brings more clarity and less frustration. But for now, I am reminded that even in a well-oiled machine, communication can always be improved. And maybe, just maybe, I am an idiot for expecting everyone to be on the same page without explicitly telling them.
